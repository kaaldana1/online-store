# Item checks

Run these on every item. They decide what you can claim, what the seller must still
photograph or measure, and whether the item should be listed at all.

## 1. Evidence for every claim

For each of brand/maker, material, purity, era and authenticity, ask: what in the photos
proves it? Record the answer in `evidence`. No proof → leave the claim out of titles and
item specifics, use "appears to be" in the description at most, and add a check asking
for the photo that would settle it.

Common photos to request (pick the one that matters, phrase it specifically):

- Close-up of a stamp, hallmark or maker's mark (clasp, ring shank, back of handle,
  underside of a base).
- Bag: creed patch or inside label, both sides; serial or style number; hardware
  engravings; corners and handle ends.
- Clothing: brand tag, size tag, fabric content and care tag, RN number.
- Any flaw mentioned in the notes but not visible.
- A ruler or tape measure next to the item when size matters.
- A kitchen scale reading in grams for silver and jewelry.

## 2. Material traps

- **Silver**: sterling marks are "STERLING", "925", ".925", "Sterling 925", the British
  lion passant, or a maker's mark that includes the word. "800", "830", "835" are
  continental silver, "900" or "COIN" is coin silver: real silver, but say the purity
  and don't call it sterling. Plate marks: "EP", "EPNS", "EPC", "A1", "Silverplate",
  "Silver on copper", "Triple Plate", "Quadruple Plate", "Sheffield" on most modern
  pieces, and maker names that are plate brands (1847 Rogers Bros, Wm. Rogers,
  Holmes & Edwards, Oneida Community). See `sterling-tableware.md`.
- **Weighted silver**: "weighted", "reinforced" or "cement" on candlesticks, compotes
  and bases means most of the weight isn't silver. Say "weighted" in the title.
- **Gold**: karat stamps are 10K/417, 14K/585, 18K/750, 22K/916. "GF", "1/20 12K GF",
  "RGP", "HGE", "GE", "vermeil" (gold over sterling) and "gold tone" are not solid
  gold. A color alone proves nothing.
- **Stones**: photos can't prove diamond, natural, or untreated. Describe color and cut;
  say "CZ" or "rhinestone" only when marked or obvious; otherwise "clear stone,
  untested".
- **Pearls**: "faux" if the finish or wear shows coating loss; otherwise "pearls,
  untested" unless documented.
- **Leather**: "genuine leather" stamps are fine to repeat; don't call a coated canvas
  "leather".

## 3. Authenticity and counterfeits

Counterfeit-prone brands in this inventory's range: Tiffany & Co. (especially "Return to
Tiffany"), David Yurman, Pandora, James Avery, Chanel/Dior/YSL costume, Coach,
Michael Kors, Kate Spade, Tory Burch, Louis Vuitton, Gucci.

- Treat them as "brand unconfirmed" until the marks look right in clear photos.
- Red flags: misspelled or poorly cut stamps, uneven or crooked stitching on labels,
  hardware stamped wrong, a style number that doesn't match the bag (see
  `handbags.md`), a "brand" on an item that brand never made.
- Strong red flags → `blockers` ("Tiffany stamp shallow and misaligned; looks
  counterfeit. Don't list as Tiffany.").
- Unbranded or unknown is fine. Many good items are unsigned; describe them by
  material, style and era.

## 4. Restricted or prohibited items

Put these in `blockers` and say why. The seller decides; your job is to flag.

- **Ivory and items that look like it** (bone and resin look-alikes included until
  proven otherwise). eBay and Facebook prohibit ivory, and many states ban its sale.
- **Tortoiseshell** from sea turtles, **rhino horn**, **whalebone/scrimshaw** of
  uncertain origin, **coral** of protected species, **feathers** from protected birds
  (eagle, hawk, owl and most native wild birds).
- **Exotic skins** (alligator, crocodile, python): legal in many places but restricted
  by some states and platforms. Flag for the seller to check.
- **Native American or tribal claims**: under the federal Indian Arts and Crafts Act,
  it is illegal to market an item as Native American made, or by a named tribe, unless
  it was made by an enrolled member or certified artisan. Without documentation (a
  hallmark you can identify as a registered artist plus provenance), don't use those
  words anywhere; use "Southwestern style". Add a check.
- **Counterfeits**: see above.
- **Recalled or unsafe items**: vintage baby gear, cribs, car seats, children's jewelry
  that may contain lead, drop-side anything. Flag.
- **Weapons**: knives other than tableware, swords, ammunition. Facebook prohibits
  weapons; flag.
- **Perfume and aerosols**: restricted for shipping; Facebook local pickup is fine.

## 5. Condition

Pick the `condition` value by these rules:

- `new with tags`: original tags attached, never used.
- `like new`: no visible wear.
- `good`: light wear, nothing that affects use.
- `fair`: visible flaws (stains, dents, missing stones, broken clasps) or repair needed.
- `for parts`: broken, or valued for material or salvage.

In `condition_notes`, list each flaw and where it is. Silver: dents, bends, tine
alignment, monogram, polishing wear on patterns, knife-blade pitting, disposal marks.
Bags: corner wear, handle darkening, interior stains, pen marks, odor (ask), peeling.
Clothing: pilling, stains, holes, fading, missing buttons, stretched collars.
