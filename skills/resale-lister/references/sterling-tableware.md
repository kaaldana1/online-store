# Sterling silver tableware (and silverplate)

Buyers here are mostly replacing pieces in a pattern they own, so the maker and pattern
name are the most important words. The second group buys by silver weight, so sterling
proof and gram weight matter too.

## First: sterling or plate?

Decide this before anything else (see `item-checks.md` for marks). Then:

- **Sterling**: say "Sterling Silver" in every title.
- **Silverplate**: say "Silverplate" or "Silver Plated" in every title, never "silver"
  alone. Plate still sells, by pattern (1847 Rogers Bros "First Love" and similar),
  but for much less.
- **Same maker, both kinds**: several makers made both. "International Sterling" is
  sterling, "International Silver Co." on its own is usually plate. Wallace,
  Reed & Barton, Towle and Oneida also made both lines. Trust the purity mark, not the
  name.
- **Hollow-handle knives and serving pieces**: the handle is sterling, the blade is
  stainless, and the handle is filled. Title as "Sterling Handle" or "Hollow Handle".
- **Weighted hollowware** (candlesticks, compotes, bud vases): say "Weighted".

## Title formula

`[Maker] [Pattern] Sterling Silver [Piece] [Length] [No Mono / Mono] [Qty]`

- `Gorham Chantilly Sterling Silver Dinner Fork 7 3/4in No Monogram`
- `Wallace Grand Baroque Sterling Silver Teaspoons Set of 6 No Mono`
- `Reed & Barton Francis I Sterling Silver Pierced Serving Spoon`
- `Towle Old Master Sterling Handle Butter Spreader Hollow Handle`
- `International Sterling Prelude 4 Piece Place Setting 1940s`

## Identifying the pattern

1. Read the back of the handle: maker's mark, "STERLING", sometimes a pattern name
   or a patent date.
2. Describe the design in words (scrolls, shells, flowers, beading, plain).
3. If the pattern isn't obvious, search maker plus design words, and compare against a
   pattern reference (Replacements, Ltd. has an extensive online pattern catalog;
   maker archives and silver-mark sites help with marks).

Well-known patterns, as a starting point only: Gorham Chantilly, Strasbourg, Buttercup;
Wallace Grand Baroque, Rose Point; Reed & Barton Francis I, Burgundy; Kirk Repousse;
Towle Old Master, King Richard; International Prelude, Joan of Arc; Lunt Eloquence.

## Piece names

Name the exact piece, because collectors search that way and the prices differ.
Measure tip to tip to tell similar pieces apart:

- Forks: dinner (largest), place, luncheon, salad, cocktail/seafood, cold meat (serving).
- Spoons: teaspoon, place/oval soup, round cream soup, bouillon, iced tea, demitasse,
  sugar, tablespoon/serving, berry, pierced serving.
- Knives: dinner and place (usually hollow handle), butter spreader (flat or hollow
  handle), master butter.
- Serving: gravy or cream ladle, pie or cake server, tomato server, cheese server,
  salad set, carving set.

If you can't tell dinner from place size from the photo, use the general name
("Fork") and add a check to measure the length.

## What to record

- Maker, pattern, piece name, quantity, length in inches.
- Weight in grams (and troy ounces = grams ÷ 31.1). Note "weighted" or "hollow handle"
  pieces don't count fully.
- Monogram: yes or no, and the letters and style if yes. "No Mono" is a valued search
  term; monograms lower the price for most buyers.
- Condition: dents, bent tines, disposal or wear marks, knife-blade pitting or
  separation at the handle, heavy polishing wear on the pattern, tarnish (normal; say
  "will polish" only if the seller confirms).
- For sets: count each piece type, chest or box included, whether pieces match.
